Hi, I am Amna Jamal, an early-career technical AI safety researcher and computer science graduate from COMSATS University Islamabad. My primary research focus is mechanistic interpretability: understanding how advanced AI systems represent and process information internally, and using causal interventions to distinguish genuine mechanisms from correlations.

My research began with studying high-norm activations and sink-register behaviour in Diffusion Transformers. I am now extending this work through projects on representation steering, concept unlearning, the downstream effects of high-norm activations, and the robustness of activation-based safety probes. I also work on a smaller number of evaluation projects concerning reasoning faithfulness in vision-language-action systems.

I am currently spending a research-focused gap year developing my research portfolio before applying to PhD programs in technical AI safety and mechanistic interpretability.

I am interested in research collaborations and future PhD opportunities related to mechanistic interpretability, representation monitoring, and technical AI safety.

Diffusion Transformers Use Sink Registers

Amna Jamal, Mika Tan, Clarissa Aurelia Nahid Saputra, Quan Huynh, Kevin Zhu, Antonio Mari

AAAI 2026 XAI4Science Workshop Accepted

• First Author
Investigated high-norm activations in Diffusion Transformers and studied their emergence and functional role using causal ablations, activation analysis, probing, and attention and MLP analysis. The work provides evidence that these activations can behave as sink registers in diffusion models.

Concept Unlearning in Diffusion Transformers via Steering Vectors

Under Review Under Review

Investigates whether activation-space steering can suppress selected visual concepts in Diffusion Transformers while preserving unrelated generation capabilities. The work evaluates the effectiveness, specificity, generalisation, and unintended effects of representation-level concept interventions.

• Research themes
Mechanistic interpretability, representation steering, concept unlearning, Diffusion Transformers

Downstream Effects of High-Norm Activations in Diffusion Transformers

Ongoing Research

Investigates whether sink registers and naturally occurring high-norm activations affect downstream methods that depend on a model’s internal activation distribution. The project examines whether unusually large activation norms distort calibration statistics or representation geometry in quantisation, pruning, distillation, model editing, and feature reuse.

A central question is whether explicitly identifying or handling register and high-norm tokens can improve compressed or modified Diffusion Transformers compared with standard activation-handling methods.

• Research themes
Mechanistic interpretability, sink registers, high-norm activations, model compression, quantisation, representation geometry

Probing the Probes

Ongoing Research

A Cross-Family Audit of Safety-Probe Robustness under Confound-Targeted Decorrelation

Investigates whether activation-based safety probes detect their intended latent properties or instead depend on superficial correlations in their training data. The project uses controlled, confound-targeted evaluations to test probe robustness across model families and safety-relevant settings involving evaluation awareness, refusal, harmful intent, and deception.

The broader aim is to assess whether linear probes are sufficiently reliable for safety monitoring when correlations present in their original evaluation distributions are deliberately altered.

• Research themes
Mechanistic interpretability, safety probes, representation monitoring, confound robustness, language-model safety

SafeDriveX: A Benchmark for Reasoning Faithfulness in Vision-Language-Action Driving Models

Ongoing Research

Developing a benchmark for evaluating whether the stated reasoning of vision-language-action driving models is consistent with the visual evidence they observe, the actions they select, and the trajectories they produce.

The work includes stronger fidelity evaluation, human-audited annotations, cross-model comparison, failure analysis, and robustness testing under controlled perturbations.

• Research themes
Vision-language-action models, reasoning faithfulness, multimodal evaluations, benchmark design, driving safety

Faithfulness-Aware Fine-Tuning for Vision-Language-Action Driving Models

Ongoing Research

Investigates whether targeted fine-tuning can improve consistency between a vision-language-action model’s generated reasoning, the visual evidence available to it, and its selected driving behaviour.

The project explores whether faithfulness-oriented supervision can reduce unsupported reasoning and improve reasoning-action consistency without degrading driving performance.

• Research themes
Vision-language-action models, faithfulness-aware fine-tuning, reasoning-action consistency, multimodal AI safety
