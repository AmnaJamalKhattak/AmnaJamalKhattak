Hi, I am Amna Jamal, a computer science graduate and an early-career researcher in AI safety with a particular focus on mechanistic interpretability.

My research is mostly about understanding what is actually happening inside modern AI systems: how they represent and process information internally, what shapes their behaviour, and how causal methods can reveal the mechanisms underlying their decisions. I am drawn to this because as AI systems grow more capable and more widely deployed, being able to look inside them and verify what they are doing, rather than just treating them as black boxes, feels like one of the most important problems to work on. We can never make something safe that we do not understand.

I am currently working on multiple research projects, and building toward PhD applications in mechanistic interpretability and technical AI safety. Alongside my own research, I help grow AI safety research capacity in Pakistan through a community that I have built called ResearchCircle. Longer term, I want to do interpretability research that makes advanced AI systems more transparent and safer to deploy.

I am open to research collaborations and PhD opportunities in these areas.

Diffusion Transformers Use Sink Registers

Amna Jamal, Mika Tan, Clarissa Aurelia Nahid Saputra, Quan Huynh, Kevin Zhu, Antonio Mari

AAAI 2026 XAI4Science Workshop Accepted

• First Author
Studied how high-norm activations emerge in Diffusion Transformers and what role they play, using causal ablation, activation analysis, probing, and attention and MLP analysis. We show that a small number of emergent high-norm tokens behave as sink registers, absorbing attention rather than encoding special semantic meaning.

Concept Unlearning in Diffusion Transformers via Steering Vectors

Under Review Under Review

Investigates whether activation-space steering can suppress selected visual concepts in Diffusion Transformers while preserving unrelated generation. Evaluates the effectiveness, specificity, and unintended effects of representation-level concept interventions.

• Research themes

Downstream Effects of High-Norm Activations in Diffusion Transformers

Ongoing Research

Investigates whether sink registers and naturally occurring high-norm activations distort downstream methods that depend on a model’s internal activation distribution, such as quantisation, pruning, distillation, and feature reuse. A central question is whether explicitly handling register and high-norm tokens can improve Diffusion Transformers over standard methods.

• Research themes
High-norm activations, model compression, quantisation, representation geometry

Probing the Probes

A Cross-Family Audit of Safety Probe Robustness under Confound Targeted Decorrelation

Investigates whether activation-based safety probes detect their intended latent properties or instead rely on superficial correlations in their training data. Uses controlled, confound-targeted evaluations to test probe robustness across model families and safety-relevant settings.

• Research themes
Safety probes, representation monitoring, confound robustness, language-model safety

SafeDriveX: A Benchmark for Reasoning Faithfulness in Vision-Language-Action Driving Models

Ongoing Research

A benchmark for evaluating whether the stated reasoning of vision-language-action driving models is consistent with the visual evidence they observe, the actions they select, and the trajectories they produce.

• Research themes

Faithfulness-Aware Fine-Tuning for Vision-Language-Action Driving Models

Ongoing Research

Investigates whether targeted, faithfulness-oriented fine-tuning can improve consistency between a vision-language-action model’s generated reasoning, the visual evidence available to it, and its driving behaviour, without degrading driving performance.

• Research themes
